# 04.8 local (cis) genotype and methylation

**Question:** 04.7 found that genome-wide relatedness (genotype PCs) explains about 8–12% of the methylation differences between oysters, and 5–9% without the four genetic outliers. Is that because nearby genetic variants control methylation locally (cis, like methylation QTLs or allele-specific methylation)? Or does it reflect something genome-wide, such as family or source? If it's local, methylation should be more strongly associated with the genotype of a SNP **near** it than with the genotype of a SNP elsewhere, and the link should weaken with distance.

## Data

- **SNP genotypes:** BS-SNPer PASS calls at CpGs (04.4 `prep-bssnper/CpG-SNP-genotypes.rds`), bisulfite-safe only (no C>T at the C, no G>A at the G).
  - **Callable in every oyster:** a site is kept only if every oyster has ≥ 10 methylation reads there, so an oyster without a SNP call can be scored as reference. The BS-SNPer VCFs list only variant calls, so SNPs away from CpGs can't be genotyped this way.
  - **Carriers:** at least 3 oysters carry the SNP and at least 3 don't (`MIN_CARRIERS`).
  - **Genotype value:** the alt allele frequency.
- **CpG methylation:** the 04.4 `noSNP` set (no SNP at the C or G in any oyster), with data in every oyster. So the SNP never directly changes a tested CpG.
- **Gene-body methylation:** 04.6 gene region counts, with ≥ 10 reads in every oyster.

## Tests

1. **SNP–CpG pairs:** every SNP against every non-SNP CpG within 50 kb, binned by distance (0–250, 250–500, 500–1,000, 1–2k, 2–5k, 5–10k, 10–20k, 20–50k bp). A smoke test at 10 kb still showed about 2× enrichment at 5–10 kb, so the window was widened to see where the decay levels off.
2. **SNP–gene pairs:** every SNP inside a gene body against that gene's methylation.

Each pair gets a partial-correlation t-test after removing the covariates from both the genotype and the methylation. There are two covariate sets:
- **Treatment:** effect-coded ploidy, pH and ploidy × pH.
- **Treatment + 3 genome-wide genotype PCs:** this asks whether local genotype matters beyond relatedness.

**Nulls,** 20 draws each. The same SNPs and pairs, but each partner CpG or gene is replaced by one drawn at random:
- **Other chromosome ("trans"):** keeps each SNP's genotype distribution and the genome-wide genetic structure, and breaks the physical link. It also breaks relatedness that runs along a chromosome. In hatchery families, oysters can share long haplotype stretches, which link genotype and methylation over many kb without any local control.
- **Same chromosome, more than 1 Mb away (`FAR_DIST`):** keeps chromosome-scale relatedness. Enrichment over this null is the part that is genuinely local. It was added after the first full run, where enrichment over the other-chromosome null stayed at about 2× out to 50 kb.
- **Statistics:** the share of pairs at p < 0.001 (cis vs. trans), the enrichment ratio, and π₁ = 1 − π₀ (Storey, λ = 0.5).
- **Sample sets:** all 24 oysters and `DropGeno4`. Genotype PCs are recomputed within each set.

## What would each outcome mean?

- **Cis enrichment that decays with distance, and remains after the genotype PCs:** local genetic control of methylation (meQTLs). That would make "genotype explains methylation variation" a mechanistic finding, and the cis pairs are candidates for allele-specific methylation.
- **No cis enrichment beyond trans:** the 04.7 genotype–methylation link is genome-wide (family or source structure), not local.
- **Enrichment only without the PCs:** the local signal is explained by relatedness.

Outputs: `analyses/Haws_04.8-cis-genotype/`.
- **All 24:** 10,286 SNPs, giving 677k SNP–CpG pairs within 50 kb and 8,465 SNP–gene pairs.
- **`DropGeno4`:** 12,139 SNPs (genotype PCs recomputed), giving 905k SNP–CpG pairs and 9,924 SNP–gene pairs.
- **Each null:** 20 draws.

The observed share is above the null's 95th percentile in every bin, for both nulls, both sample sets, and both covariate sets. Without the genotype PCs, enrichment over the other-chromosome null is higher (2.4–5.4×). The full tables, including π₁, are in `cis-CpG-by-distance.csv` and `cis-gene.csv`.

**Interpretation:**
- **Methylation is linked to the genotype of nearby SNPs, beyond genome-wide relatedness.** This holds with all 24 and without the four outliers, for single CpGs and gene bodies, with or without the genotype PCs.
- **About a third of the other-chromosome enrichment is chromosome-scale relatedness.** The same-chromosome null has a higher baseline (0.19% vs. 0.13%), as expected if oysters share long haplotype stretches along chromosomes, which hatchery families do.
- **Over the same-chromosome null, the remaining enrichment has two parts:** an extra peak within 1 kb of the SNP (2.4–2.8× with all 24, 1.7–2.1× without the outliers), and a flat 1.5–1.9× from 1 kb out to 50 kb. The LD check below shows that only the peak matches the genetic linkage.

**Caveat on significance:** neighbouring CpGs are correlated, so observed pairs near the same SNP aren't independent, while the null pairs are scattered. The mean enrichment is unbiased, but the null 95th percentiles understate how variable the observed share is. Treat the 95th-percentile comparison as descriptive. The consistency across bins, sample sets, and nulls is the stronger evidence.

### LD decay

r² between the genotypes of every pair of the same SNPs on the same chromosome, against 200,000 random pairs on different chromosomes (`LD-decay.csv`, `LD-decay.png`). With 24 oysters, r² ≈ 1/(n − 1) = 0.043 is expected from sampling alone. Mean r², raw genotypes, all 24:

| Distance | Pairs | Mean r² |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 kb | 2,546 | 0.077 |
| 1–5 kb | 3,149 | 0.058 |
| 20–50 kb | 8,772 | 0.061 |
| 100 kb–1 Mb | 182,427 | 0.060 |
| 1–5 Mb | 731,730 | 0.059 |
| 5–10 Mb | 784,101 | 0.056 |
| > 10 Mb | 3.7M | 0.050 |
| other chromosome | 200,000 | 0.045 |

`DropGeno4` and the PC-adjusted genotypes show the same shape. The values are slightly higher, because fewer oysters and fewer residual degrees of freedom raise the sampling baseline.

- **Linkage between nearby SNPs is short.** Only SNPs within about 1 kb are in clearly higher LD.
- **Beyond 1 kb there is a weak, flat excess** (r² ≈ 0.06 vs. 0.045) that stays almost the same from 1 kb to 5 Mb and fades only beyond 10 Mb. That is chromosome-scale relatedness, such as long shared (IBD) segments in hatchery families. It isn't haplotype blocks of tens of kb.

**What this means for the cis result:**
- **The peak within 1 kb is consistent with local (cis) control of methylation.** It sits exactly where the only real short-range LD is. Methylation near a SNP tracks that SNP's genotype, or a variant tightly linked to it (methylation QTLs or allele-specific methylation).
- **The flat 1.5–1.9× from 1 to 50 kb is not explained by LD.** LD at 20–50 kb (0.061) is almost the same as at 1–5 Mb (0.059), the range the same-chromosome null samples, but the methylation association is 1.6× stronger. That correction to the earlier "haplotype blocks" reading was made on 2026-10-02.
  - **More likely: methylation varies in regional blocks.** Methylation co-varies across neighbouring CpGs over tens of kb (gene-body methylation domains). One local genetic effect on a domain would then show up at every CpG in it.
  - **Other possibilities:** a regional technical effect, such as coverage or mappability that varies with local sequence, or a more complex local genetic architecture.
- **Checked next (below):** whether methylation co-varies over tens of kb, and whether conditioning on the methylation near the SNP removes the plateau.

### Methylation correlation decay and conditioning on local methylation

**Correlation decay** (`methylation-correlation-decay.csv`, `methylation-correlation-decay.png`), after removing treatment and the genotype PCs. Anchors are 20,000 random CpGs, each paired with every CpG within 100 kb. Mean correlation of methylation between CpG pairs:

| Distance | 0–250 bp | 0.5–1 kb | 1–2 kb | 5–10 kb | 20–50 kb | 50–100 kb | same chr, > 1 Mb | other chr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All 24 | 0.40 | 0.18 | 0.15 | 0.11 | 0.056 | 0.040 | 0.026 | 0.025 |
| `DropGeno4` | 0.36 | 0.14 | 0.11 | 0.067 | 0.036 | 0.025 | 0.011 | 0.011 |

Methylation does vary in regional blocks. Correlation between CpGs stays above the far baseline out to 50–100 kb, which is the same range as the cis plateau and far beyond LD (about 1 kb). The small positive baseline is shared by every pair, consistent with oyster-wide differences in overall methylation level.

**Conditioning test** (`cis-conditioned-on-local-methylation.csv`). For SNPs with at least one CpG within 1 kb, SNP–CpG pairs 1–50 kb apart were retested with the mean methylation of those nearby CpGs as an extra covariate, against the same-chromosome (> 1 Mb) null treated the same way:

| Sample set | SNPs | Pairs | Enrichment, unconditioned | Enrichment, conditioned on methylation within 1 kb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All 24 | 3,321 | 376k | 1.45× | 1.37× |
| `DropGeno4` | 4,060 | 516k | 1.51× | 1.35× |

- **Conditioning on methylation near the SNP removes only about 15–30% of the excess.** Most of the plateau remains.
- **That doesn't rule out the domain explanation.** The local proxy is a mean of a few noisy CpGs measured in 24 oysters: CpGs within 250 bp correlate only about 0.4 with each other. So conditioning on it removes only part of a domain-wide effect. A domain effect anchored elsewhere in the domain, not at the SNP, also wouldn't be captured.

**Where this leaves the plateau.** Three things are now established:
- Methylation co-varies over the same range as the plateau.
- Genotype linkage doesn't extend that far.
- Conditioning on the methylation right at the SNP leaves most of the plateau.

The most consistent reading is that a SNP's local effect (or an effect of something tightly linked to it) reaches methylation across a regional domain, and the local proxy is too noisy to absorb it. **An oyster-specific alternative is structural variation.** *C. gigas* has extensive presence/absence and hemizygous regions spanning kb to tens of kb. A SNP inside such a region could tag its copy-number state. That would change both the apparent genotype and the methylation and coverage of nearby CpGs, without ordinary LD.
- **A cheap test:** does SNP genotype predict the **coverage** of nearby CpGs, beyond the trans and far nulls? If it does, structural variation is a likely contributor.

**How this connects to 04.7:** part of the genotype–methylation link (04.7) is local, a sharp effect within about 1 kb that probably spreads across regional methylation domains. The rest is chromosome- and genome-scale relatedness. Methylation differences between these oysters partly follow local genotype.

## Limitations

- **Only SNPs at CpGs**, and only those well covered in every oyster. These are a sparse, biased sample of the variants near each CpG.
- **24 oysters, about 16 residual degrees of freedom.** Individual pair p-values are weak, so the test relies on enrichment over many pairs. Single pairs aren't reported as discoveries.
- **Linkage across chromosomes is assumed negligible.** The trans null treats different chromosomes as unlinked. Strong family structure could still link them, but the genotype PCs absorb most of that.
- **Mappability:** a SNP could change read mapping near it, and so the methylation estimate. The noSNP filter and the ≥ 10-read requirement limit this but don't rule it out.
