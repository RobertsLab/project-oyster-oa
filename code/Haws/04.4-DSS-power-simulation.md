# 04.4 DSS power simulation

**Question:** 04.4 (DSS) and 04.6 (methylKit regions) found no ploidy or pH effect that can be told apart from label permutations. How large would a real effect have to be for this experiment to detect it? The answer turns "no effect found" into a stated detection limit. See [04-methylation-summary.md](04-methylation-summary.md), option A.

## Approach: spike known effects into the real data

Simulating from a fitted model would assume the beta-binomial is right, and the permutations show it isn't quite (DSS's nominal FDR is too optimistic). So the effects are added to the **real counts** instead. That keeps each oyster's own variation, the genetic outliers, the coverage, and the missing data.

- **Base data:** the primary setting, `cov5-hiperc99.9-cell5-meth10-All` (558,833 CpGs, 24 oysters), with the **real labels**. That keeps the real design, including the four genetic outliers being in high pH. Real-label "DML" are within the permutation null, so they are counted as false hits.
- **Spikes:** for each replicate, choose 1,000 nuclear CpGs for ploidy and another 1,000 for pH (0.2% each). They come from CpGs with mean methylation 20–80%, so every effect size can be applied the same way. Each CpG gets a random direction.
- **Binomial thinning:** at a spiked CpG with mean methylation *m*, oysters on the "up" side have each unmethylated read switched to methylated with probability (Δ/2)/(1 − *m*). Oysters on the "down" side have each methylated read switched with probability (Δ/2)/*m*. The group means move apart by Δ on average, coverage doesn't change, and each oyster keeps its deviation from its group. A synthetic check gave shifts of 10.06 and 9.83 for Δ = 10, and 40.15 and 40.06 for Δ = 40.
- **Ploidy effects** are spiked between triploids and diploids, balanced over pH. **pH effects** are spiked between low and high, balanced over ploidy. Because of the effect coding, one doesn't leak into the other's test.
- **Grid:** Δ ∈ {5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40} percentage points × 10 replicates = 70 DSS fits. Each fit takes ~2 min. Within a replicate, the same CpGs are spiked at every Δ, so the power curves are paired.

## Detection rules

1. **DSS FDR < 0.05.** Nominal; reported for reference, since it's too optimistic for this data.
2. **Empirical FDR < 0.05.** The largest p-value cutoff where (mean count below it across the 100 label permutations) / (count below it in the simulated data) ≤ 0.05. This is the cutoff a permutation-calibrated analysis would use. **This is the main per-CpG power number.**
3. **Experiment-level detection.** Is the run's total DML count (DSS FDR < 0.05) above the 95th percentile of the 100 permutation counts? That's the test 04.4 actually applied to the real data. It asks whether we would have concluded that there is an effect.

Also recorded: power at FDR < 0.05 with an observed |Δ| ≥ 10% or ≥ 25%, sign agreement, false hits, and the observed Δ at the spiked CpGs.

## How to run

```bash
OUT_DIR=<checkout with prep files>/analyses/Haws_04.4-DSS   # only needed from a git worktree
for d in 5 10 15 20 25 30 40; do for r in $(seq 1 10); do
  SIM_DELTA=$d SIM_REP=$r bash 04.4-DSS-slurm-submit.sh
done; done
bash 04.4-DSS-slurm-submit.sh simsummary
```

Outputs go to `analyses/Haws_04.4-DSS/power-simulation/`: `power-runs-*.csv` (one row per run × term), `power-summary-*.csv` (mean over replicates), and `power-curves-*.png`.

## Limitations and possible extensions

- **CpG level only.** Real oyster methylation changes may cover gene bodies or blocks. A region-level version (spike every CpG in a 1 kb window or gene, test with methylKit `MN` regions from 04.6) would give the detection limit for that kind of effect. It's the natural next step if the CpG-level limit is high.
- **Number of affected CpGs:** 1,000 per term. With more true effects, the empirical-FDR cutoff relaxes, because true hits dilute the permutation counts. `SIM_N` can test 5,000 or 10,000.
- **Effects are uniform within a group.** Real effects probably vary between oysters, which would lower power further. So these numbers are a best case.
- **Spiked CpGs are 20–80% methylated.** Those are the CpGs where a difference can show up. Most oyster CpGs are near 0% or 100%.
