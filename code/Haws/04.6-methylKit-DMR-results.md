# 04.6 methylKit DMR results

Results of the region-level methylKit analysis planned in [04.6-methylKit-DMR-plan.md](04.6-methylKit-DMR-plan.md), run 2026-10-02 with [04.6-methylKit-DMR-slurm.R](04.6-methylKit-DMR-slurm.R). Outputs are in `analyses/Haws_04.6-methylKit-DMR/`.

## Summary

**With overdispersion modeled and label permutations as the null, methylKit finds no region-level effect of ploidy or pH**, whether the regions are 250 bp windows, 1 kb windows, or whole genes. Gene-set enrichment (GSEA with NCBI GO terms) finds none either. The real labels give about as many hits as shuffled labels at every threshold tested.

This agrees with the per-CpG results in 04.3 and the DSS results in 04.4: at n = 6 oysters per ploidy × pH group, neither treatment produces methylation differences that can be told apart from differences between individual oysters.

**The genetic structure found in 04.4 doesn't change this.** That's four genetically distinct oysters, all at high pH. The result is the same with those oysters removed, three different ways, and with a permutation null that keeps them in place. The one weak pH lead (9 genes at q < 0.05 with 3 outliers dropped) disappears when all four are dropped. The top all-24 pH gene set weakens from FDR 0.078 to 0.275 under the genotype-matched null. See [Does the genetic structure found in 04.4 change this?](#does-the-genetic-structure-found-in-044-change-this)

Two methodological findings matter for any further methylation analysis of this data:

1. **Without overdispersion (`none`), methylKit reports thousands of DMRs**, 4,539–9,056 at q < 0.05 and |Δ| ≥ 10%. These reflect differences between individual oysters, not treatment.
2. **With overdispersion (`MN`), methylKit's q-values are still too optimistic.** With shuffled labels, BH q < 0.05 flags 27–185 windows in *every* permutation, where at most about 1 in 20 permutations should flag anything. Significance for this data has to come from permutations, not from methylKit q-values.

## Methods in brief

- **Data:** the 24 Bismark coverage files (Roslin assembly), all CpGs with at least 1 read, about 10M per oyster.
- **High-coverage filter:** CpGs above each oyster's 99.9th-percentile coverage are removed (cutoffs 55–102x), calculated on nuclear CpGs only. Mito CpGs are kept, unlike 04.3, which drops them all. `methRead` was called with `header = FALSE`; the default treats the first CpG of each file as a header and drops it.
- **Regions:**
  - **Windows:** non-overlapping 250 bp and 1 kb windows with at least 3 covered CpGs.
  - **Genes:** whole gene bodies from `cgigas_uk_roslin_v1_gene.gff`.
  - **Coverage:** at least 10 reads per region in each oyster.
  - **Presence:** coverage in all 24 oysters (primary), with at least 10 or 8 per group as checks.
- **Test:** `calculateDiffMeth` with `overdispersion = "MN"` (F-test). The other factor is a covariate, so this is the additive model `~ ploidy + pH`; methylKit can't include the ploidy × pH interaction.
- **Negative control:** the same tests with `overdispersion = "none"`.
- **DMR definition (fixed before any results):** q < 0.05 and |Δ| ≥ 10 percentage points. Counts at q < 0.01, |Δ| ≥ 15/25%, and a q-only tier (any |Δ|) were also recorded. The q-only tier was added after the first results.
- **Permutations:** ploidy is shuffled within pH and pH within ploidy, using the same code and seeds as 04.4 DSS. That gives 20 for windows and 100 for genes.
- **Enrichment:** genes are ranked by signed −log10 p. NCBI GO terms (`gene_ontology.gaf.gz`, with GO.db ancestors added) are kept for sets of 10–500 genes, giving 2,196 sets. Each set gets a GSEA enrichment score. NES, nominal p, and FDR all come from the 100 label permutations. Each permutation was also scored against the others, as a calibration check.

### Why there are no DMRs: significance and effect size don't overlap

- **Regions that pass q < 0.05 differ by very little.** The median |Δ| among windows with p < 0.001 is about 1 percentage point. These are well-covered regions where a small shift can be measured precisely.
- **Regions with large differences aren't significant.** Thousands of regions differ by ≥ 10%, but they're noisy, low-coverage regions that the overdispersion model correctly doesn't trust.
- **Conversion efficiency doesn't explain the small shifts.** Bisulfite conversion differs between groups by only about 0.01 percentage points (99.50% in every group).

### P-value calibration

- **Overall shape:** p-value histograms are close to flat. 5.5–6.6% of p-values are below 0.05, and shuffled labels give the same share (5.5–7.0%).
- **The extreme tail:** a small number of regions get extremely small p-values (down to about 1e-12, with |Δ| about 1%) whether the labels are real or shuffled. That's why BH q < 0.05 flags windows in every permutation. The likely cause is well-covered regions where methylKit's overdispersion estimate falls to its floor. This hasn't been confirmed yet.

- **The one pH set:** *mitotic spindle assembly checkpoint signaling* (GO:0007094, 12 genes, NES −1.95, FDR 0.078). Its genes are 2.2 points less methylated at low pH on average.
- **It isn't distinguishable from chance:** about half of the shuffled labelings also give at least one set at FDR < 0.25.
- **Its neighbours aren't independent support:** nearby sets (nuclear division, chromosome segregation, sister chromatid separation) lean the same way, but they share its genes.
- **The FDR estimate runs liberal:** most shuffled labelings flag nothing, but a few flag hundreds of sets (up to 652 for ploidy and 1,168 for pH).

## Does the genetic structure found in 04.4 change this?

The 04.4 SNP check (`analyses/Haws_04.4-DSS/SNP-check/`) found that **genetic background accounts for much of the methylation difference between oysters**:

- **Genotype tracks methylation:** genotype similarity tracks methylation similarity across oyster pairs (rho = 0.86).
- **Four genetic outliers:** 2H-1, 2H-2, 3H-5 and 3H-2 separate in both the genotype and methylation PCAs.
- **All four are high-pH oysters.** If they were spread at random, that would happen about 5% of the time. Two are diploid and two triploid, so ploidy is balanced.

**Ploidy: unlikely to change.** The outliers are split 2/2 between diploids and triploids, and among the other 20 oysters genotype isn't related to treatment. DSS with genotype PC1 as a covariate still finds no ploidy effect (265 observed vs. a permuted median of 212).

**pH: it could change, and the current analysis doesn't settle it.** The four genetic outliers can affect a pH test in two opposite ways:

1. **False signal.** If genotype shifts their methylation, a high-vs-low comparison is partly "these four oysters vs. the rest". That would inflate the real pH count above the permutations. It didn't happen: real pH counts are at or below the permutation median. So the outliers aren't creating false DMRs here.
2. **Hidden signal.** The outliers add between-oyster variation, all of it in the high-pH group. `MN` treats that variation as noise, so the pH test loses power. A real but modest pH effect could be masked. Dropping the outliers is the way to find out.

The permutations also aren't a perfect null for pH. They spread the four outliers across both pH groups at random, but the real labels have all four in high pH.

**What wouldn't help:**
- **A genotype covariate for pH.** Genotype PC1 is confounded with pH, so adjusting for it removes pH signal along with the genetic effect. In DSS, pH with genotype PC1 gave 65 observed vs. a permuted median of 144, below chance.
- **Masking SNP CpGs.** In DSS, CpGs with SNPs don't explain the null (noSNP: 146 observed vs. 153 permuted for ploidy). At the region level, a few SNP CpGs are diluted further.

**Check run (2026-10-02).** 1 kb windows and genes were rerun without the genetic outliers, each set with its own permutations (20 for windows, 100 for genes). The all-24 analysis was also rerun with a restricted permutation, `fixGeno4`: the four outliers keep their real labels, and only the other 20 oysters are shuffled. That matches the real genotype–pH link exactly.

**Conclusion: accounting for the genetic structure doesn't change the result.** Neither removing the genetic outliers nor using a null that keeps them in place reveals a ploidy or pH effect.

- **The one lead didn't replicate.** `DropPC2` had 9 genes at q < 0.05 for pH against a permutation median of 1 (p = 0.02). They're small shifts; none reaches |Δ| ≥ 10%. The lead is one of about 70 comparisons in this check, and it's gone in `DropGeno4` (0 genes, which also drops 3H-2). So it's most likely chance, not a pH effect masked by the outliers.
- **The all-24 pH gene set was partly driven by the genetic outliers.** *Mitotic spindle assembly checkpoint signaling* weakens from FDR 0.078 to 0.275 under the null that keeps the four outliers in high pH. It falls out of the top sets when they are removed (`DropPC2`, `DropGeno4`).
- **The restricted null was the more appropriate one for pH, and it changes nothing.** The `fixGeno4` distributions are close to the free ones, and the real counts sit near the middle of both.
- **Removing oysters costs power.** `DropGeno4` keeps only 8 high-pH oysters, so a null there means "no detectable effect", not "no effect".

## Limitations

- **No interaction term:** methylKit can't fit the ploidy × pH interaction, so regions where the effect differs between the two groups of the other factor could be missed. The DSS factorial model covers that case.
- **Whole-gene tests dilute effects:** the median gene differs by only 0.3 points. The exon, intron, upstream and TE region sets are built but not yet tested.
- **Permutation numbers:** windows have 20 permutations and genes 100. Nominal GSEA p-values can't go below about 0.02, because each set is compared only with permutations whose score has the same sign (about half).
- **The overdispersion floor explanation is unconfirmed:** the cause of the extreme p-values in a few regions hasn't been checked directly.
